# Forecasting-Primary-Healthcare-Demand-Statistical-vs.-Machine-Learning-Models

This project, developed as part of the Applied Forecasting Methods course in the Master's in Data Analytics and Decision Support Systems at Coimbra Business School (ISCAC), presents an end-to-end comparative time series forecasting study on daily primary healthcare demand across the Central Region of Portugal.

The primary objective was to model and project patient visits for flu-like syndrome during seasonal peak periods, benchmarking classical statistical methods against modern machine learning regressors across a 10-day forecast horizon while directly accounting for an operational 3-day data latency gap present in the Portuguese National Health Service (SNS) reporting system.

Using daily public consultation records from the SNS Transparency Portal through January 2026, the data preparation pipeline implemented temporal interpolation to address gaps, series adjustments to guarantee strictly positive counts for multiplicative seasonal decomposition ($m=7$), and the engineering of calendar-specific exogenous features, including a consolidated non-working day indicator (`nao_util`) for weekends, national holidays, sector strikes, and bridge holidays, alongside day-of-week indexing (`dia_semana`).

The modeling phase evaluated three progressive training scenarios (a short 6-month baseline from July 2025, a complete annual cycle from September 2024, and an extended 10-quarter history from September 2023) using rolling-window cross-validation with daily increments. 

Across all benchmarks, statistical approaches consistently demonstrated superior stability over machine learning models under strong weekly seasonality. The univariate AutoARIMA model delivered the strongest global performance in the extended historical scenario, achieving the lowest overall error (MAE = 36.81 and RMSE = 48.45). Exogenous variables proved essential in mitigating error during limited-data conditions (reducing AutoARIMA MAE from 62.86 to 48.22 in Scenario 1), whereas extensive historical windows enabled the baseline statistical architecture to infer recurrent calendar dynamics autonomously. Conversely, tree-based models (XGBoost and Random Forest) exhibited high variance and severe underfitting on restricted sample sizes, stabilizing only when supplied with multi-year temporal depth. 

Together, this project demonstrates end-to-end time series expertise in Python utilizing the Nixtla ecosystem (StatsForecast, MLForecast, UtilsForecast) alongside Scikit-Learn, Pandas, and Statsmodels, highlighting robust experimental design, calendar feature engineering, and the capacity to derive actionable operational insights for healthcare capacity management.
